Rutherford's Planetary Atom

Electrons orbit central positive nucleus like planets around the Sun

Geiger–Marsden Experiment (1911)

Bombarding an ultra-thin gold foil ($\approx 400\text{ atoms thick}$) with energetic $\alpha$-particles ($^4_2\text{He}^{2+}$) disproved Thomson’s uniform "Plum Pudding" model:

  • >99.8% undeviated: Atom is overwhelmingly empty space.
  • Few moderate deflections: Positive charge is concentrated in a tiny central core.
  • 1 in 8,000 bounce back (>90°): The entire positive charge and mass is packed in a dense nucleus ($r \sim 10^{-15}\text{ m}$ vs atom $10^{-10}\text{ m}$).
Impact Parameter & Scattering Formula:
$$b = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Z e \cdot 2e}{2 K} \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$
$b \to 0 \implies \theta \to 180^\circ$ (Head-on recoil). Distance of closest approach $r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2Ze^2}{K}$.

Limitation 2: Continuous vs Line Spectrum

Classical Model Expectation: Continuous Spectrum

Since radius $r$ would decrease continuously, orbital frequency $\nu_{\text{orb}} = \frac{v}{2\pi r}$ must sweep continuously through all frequencies, predicting a continuous rainbow.

Actual Experimental Discovery: Discrete Line Spectrum

Real atoms only radiate sharply defined discrete frequencies (e.g. Balmer lines of Hydrogen)!

The Double Failure of Rutherford's Model

1. Inability to explain Atomic Stability

Maxwell's electrodynamics dictate that any accelerating charge ($a = v^2/r$) radiates power:

$$P = \frac{e^2 a^2}{6\pi \varepsilon_0 c^3} = \frac{e^2}{6\pi \varepsilon_0 c^3} \left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 m_e r^2}\right)^2$$

Lifespan $t \approx \frac{m_e^2 c^3 r_0^3}{4 e^4 / (4\pi\varepsilon_0)^2} \sim 10^{-11}\text{ s}$. Matter would not exist!

2. Inability to explain Rydberg Line Formula

Classical planetary models could not derive the discrete empirical Rydberg series formula:

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)$$

Bohr's Three Fundamental Postulates (1913)

POSTULATE 1 Stationary Non-Radiating Orbits

Electrons revolve only in specific stable orbits without radiating electromagnetic energy, overcoming classical electrodynamic collapse.

POSTULATE 2 Quantization of Angular Momentum

Only those orbits are allowed where orbital angular momentum is an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi} = \hbar$:

$$L = m_e v r_n = n \frac{h}{2\pi} = n\hbar \quad (n = 1, 2, 3\dots)$$
Radius: $r_n = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\text{ \AA}$
Energy: $E_n = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\text{ eV}$
POSTULATE 3 Bohr's Frequency Condition

Photon absorption or emission occurs only during discontinuous quantum jumps between two stationary levels:

$$\Delta E = E_i - E_f = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \implies \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H \left(\frac{1}{n_f^2} - \frac{1}{n_i^2}\right)$$

Historical & Mathematical Foundations of Quantum Mechanics

1. Rutherford Alpha Scattering (1911)

Using the classical hyperbolic Coulomb trajectory, Rutherford proved that positive charge is concentrated in a minute volume ($10^{-15}\text{ m}$) rather than spread across $10^{-10}\text{ m}$.

2. The Classical Electrodynamic Collapse

Accelerated charges radiate energy via Larmor's power equation $P = \frac{e^2 a^2}{6\pi \varepsilon_0 c^3}$. Thus, any planetary electron must spiral into the nucleus in fraction of a nanosecond, which directly contradicts the stability of matter.

3. Bohr's Quantum Leap (1913)

Niels Bohr resolved both stability and discrete spectra by proposing that angular momentum is quantized in discrete steps ($L = n\hbar$). In these orbits, electromagnetic radiation is forbidden until an electron transitions between states.
