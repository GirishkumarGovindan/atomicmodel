Double Failure of Classical Model

Accelerating electron ($a = v^2/r$) continuously radiates:

$$P = \frac{e^2 a^2}{6\pi \varepsilon_0 c^3}$$

Predicted collapse time $t \sim 10^{-11}\text{ s}$. Matter could not exist.

Bohr's Three Fundamental Postulates (1913)

P1 Stationary Orbits

Electrons revolve in non-radiating stationary orbits without losing energy.

P2 Angular Momentum Quantization

Angular momentum is an integral multiple of $\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}$:

$$L = m_e v r_n = n \frac{h}{2\pi} = n\hbar$$
$r_n = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\text{ \AA}$
$E_n = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\text{ eV}$
P3 Bohr Frequency Condition

Radiation occurs only during discrete quantum transitions:

$$\Delta E = E_i - E_f = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Atomic Physics Companion

Historical & Mathematical Foundations

1. Rutherford Alpha Scattering (1911)

Using hyperbolic Coulomb scattering, Rutherford proved positive atomic charge resides in a tiny nucleus ($10^{-15}\text{ m}$) rather than spread across $10^{-10}\text{ m}$.

2. Classical Electrodynamic Collapse

Accelerated charges radiate power via Larmor's equation $P = \frac{e^2 a^2}{6\pi \varepsilon_0 c^3}$. An electron must continuously lose energy and collapse within $10^{-11}\text{ s}$, conflicting with stable matter.

3. Bohr's Quantum Leap (1913)

Niels Bohr resolved stability and discrete spectra by introducing angular momentum quantization ($L = n\hbar$). In stationary orbits, radiation is forbidden until discontinuous transitions occur.
